Show every section at about half its final length (the full 10-minute outline is in the [presentation guide](../../project/templates/presentation-guide.md)). The point is to test the **story** and the **timing**, not to cover every detail.

| Section | Time | Ask yourselves |

| Problem, user, and question | 0:45 | Could a non-engineer say who this is for and what it decides? |
| Data and its limits | 0:45 | Source, size, units — and what the data does *not* cover? |

| Responsible AI | 0:40 | Biggest risk; where it should not be used; who is accountable? |
| Takeaway | 0:20 | One sentence a project manager would remember. |

## Roles

| Role | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **Presenters** | The presenting team | Give the 5-minute talk. Every member speaks. Afterward, listen — don't defend. Ask clarifying questions only. |
| **Timekeeper** | One member of the team that presented **just before** (slot 1 uses the last team) | Starts the clock at the first word. Holds up the cards: **"1 min"** at 4:00, **"30 s"** at 4:30, **"Stop"** at 5:00. Ends the talk at 5:30 even mid-sentence. Then times the feedback window (45 s silent writing + 2 min spoken). Records the actual time on the capture sheet. |
| **Note-taker** | Another member of the team that presented just before | Writes down every piece of spoken feedback on the **capture sheet** (in the form) and hands it to the presenting team. Talks in feedback are fast — the presenting team will not remember them otherwise. |
| **Reviewers** | After you present, your team reviews the **next two teams**. The first team is reviewed by the last two. | Each reviewer completes one **peer-feedback form** for the presenting team, so each team gets 6–8 detailed forms. |
| **Everyone else** | All other students | Watch, ask a question in the feedback window if there is time. |
| **Instructor (and TA)** | — | Gives the top one or two fixes, in writing too, and scores the dry-run rubric. |

## Schedule

A 75-minute lab fits **up to 7 teams per room** at 9 minutes each. With more than 7 teams, the class splits into two parallel groups ("pods"), one led by the instructor and one by the TA, each with its own room or corner and its own schedule. Presentation order is drawn at random at the start of lab.

## How to give useful feedback

- **Be specific and point to the slide.** "Slide 6: the MAE has no units — add MPa" is more useful than "results were unclear."
- **Tie it to the rubric.** Name the criterion: problem and users, data and limits, method and baseline, results and evaluation, verification, responsible AI, delivery, or Q&A.
- **Give the most important fix first.** One big fix is more useful than ten small ones.
- **Watch for overclaiming.** Did they say the tool is "accurate" or "ready" without evidence? Say so, kindly.
- **Be kind and honest.** Critique the work, not the person.
