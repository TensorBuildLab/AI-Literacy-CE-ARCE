## Description of Course

This course teaches the foundations of artificial intelligence (AI) and how to use AI responsibly in civil and architectural engineering (CE/ARCE) coursework and practice. Students explore the AI landscape, machine learning, neural networks, generative AI, and AI agents, and examine their applications, tools, responsible use, ethics, and policy in CE/ARCE. Every week pairs a lecture with a hands-on computer lab in Python (Google Colab) using real engineering data — concrete mixes, building energy, bridges, traffic, and construction-safety rules. Students learn to understand core AI concepts, run and interpret AI models, verify AI outputs, and recognize their limitations.

## Course Prerequisites

None. No prior programming experience is required; the labs start from the very first line of Python.

## Course Format and Teaching Methods

The class meets twice a week for 75 minutes. **Lecture day:** an interactive lecture with CE/ARCE examples, a short concept quiz on the previous week, and in-class activities. **Lab day:** a guided computer lab in Google Colab (bring a laptop) that ends with an **exit ticket** — three short questions you answer alone, without AI, in the last 10 minutes of lab. Other methods: individual homework, a team project, class discussions, and web-delivered course content.

## Course Objectives

During this course, students will:

- Attend lectures and discussions to learn the fundamental concepts of artificial intelligence, machine learning, neural networks, generative AI, and AI agents, and their applications in civil and architectural engineering.
- Perform hands-on computer lab exercises that progress from basic Python programming and working with data to running machine learning, neural network, generative AI, and agent models and understanding how they work.
- Reason about the responsible use, verification, ethics, and policy of AI in civil and architectural engineering coursework and practice.
- Engage in project-based learning to apply AI to civil and architectural engineering problems.

## Midterm Exam

The midterm is a closed-book, in-class exam held during the Week 7 lab session (75 minutes). It covers Weeks 1–6: the AI landscape, machine learning, and neural networks. It emphasizes conceptual understanding and short calculations over memorization; a formula sheet is provided and a basic calculator is allowed (any non-graphing, non-programmable calculator; no phones, laptops, or AI tools). A study guide and practice exam are posted on the course GitHub page. Make-up exams are given only for documented reasons, arranged in advance when possible.

## Final Project

Teams of 3–4 students apply AI to a civil or architectural engineering problem in one of four tracks: **Predict** (machine learning on engineering data), **See** (computer vision), **Assist** (a generative-AI or agent tool for an engineering workflow, with evaluation), or **Evaluate** (a systematic test of an AI tool on an engineering task). Every project includes a baseline, an evaluation, verification of results, a responsible-AI analysis, and an AI-use statement.

Milestones: team registration (Week 10), proposal and team charter (Week 11, 10% of the project grade), progress check-in (Week 12 or 13 lab day, in two waves, 10%), dry run with peer feedback (Week 15, 5%), final presentation (30%), final notebook and report of at most 6 pages (35%), and individual reflection and peer evaluation (10%). Individual grades may be adjusted based on peer evaluation, using the rule published in the project guide.

The final presentations take place during the final exam period: about 10 minutes per team plus questions. With more than eight teams, presentations run in two parallel rooms or as 8-minute talks; the schedule is posted by Week 13. The Final Exam Regulations and Final Exam Schedule can be found at [https://registrar.arizona.edu/finals](https://registrar.arizona.edu/finals).

## Use of Generative AI: Sometimes Permitted (by Assignment Label)

This course is about AI, so using AI is part of the learning — under clear rules. Every assignment carries one of three labels:

- **AI-Free** — do not use AI tools to produce what you submit. Applies to quizzes, the midterm exam, lab exit tickets, and homework parts labeled AI-Free. You may still use AI to study the concepts beforehand.
- **AI-Assisted** — you may use AI tools for help such as explanations, debugging, and brainstorming. Include an AI-use acknowledgment; you are responsible for every line you submit. This is the default for labs, most homework parts, and the final project.
- **AI-Required** — using an AI tool is part of the task (for example, testing a language model's answers). Follow the instructions and document your prompts and the outputs.

In all cases, you may NOT: submit AI-generated work as your own without acknowledgment; use AI to generate answers in place of participating in class discussions, debates, or activities; let AI write, analyze, or interpret the final project to the point that the work no longer reflects your team's own understanding; or paste other people's private information, exam content, or confidential data into AI tools.

**How to write an acknowledgment statement:** "I acknowledge the use of [name of AI system(s)] to [describe the specific use]. Prompts used included [share a link or copy-and-paste the prompts]. I checked the output by [how I verified it]. I am responsible for this work." See the UA Libraries guide Citing generative AI: [https://libguides.library.arizona.edu/students-chatgpt/citing](https://libguides.library.arizona.edu/students-chatgpt/citing).

If you are unsure whether a use is allowed, ask before you submit. Undisclosed or prohibited AI use is a violation of the Code of Academic Integrity.
