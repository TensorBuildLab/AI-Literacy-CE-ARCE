# Final Project Rubrics

> **What is graded, when, and points** (all times are Arizona time)
> 1. **Proposal + team charter** — Sunday of Week 11, 11:59 pm — 20 points (team)
> 2. **Progress check-in** — Week 12 lab (wave A) or Week 13 lab (wave B) — 20 points (team)
> 3. **Dry run + peer feedback** — Week 15 lab — 10 points (6 team + 4 individual)
> 4. **Final presentation** — Week 16 — 60 points (team) · **Notebook + report** — Week 16 — 70 points (team)
> 5. **Reflection + peer evaluation** — Week 16 — 20 points (individual) · **Total 200 points = 25% of the course grade**

Each milestone is scored in points: **2 points for every 1% of project weight, 200 points in total.** Your project grade is (points earned ÷ 200) × 100%.

| Milestone | When | Weight (of project) | Points | Graded as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Team formation (registration) | Sunday of Week 10 | – | – | required, not graded |
| [1. Proposal (with team charter)](#1-proposal--20-points) | Sunday of Week 11 | 10% | 20 | team |
| [2. Progress check-in](#2-progress-check-in--20-points) | Week 12 or Week 13 lab | 10% | 20 | team |
| [3. Dry run and peer feedback](#3-dry-run-and-peer-feedback--10-points) | Week 15 lab | 5% | 10 | 6 team + 4 individual |
| [4. Final presentation](#4-final-presentation--60-points) | Week 16 | 30% | 60 | team |
| [5. Final notebook and report](#5-final-notebook-and-report--70-points) | Week 16 | 35% | 70 | team |
| [6. Individual reflection and peer evaluation](#6-individual-reflection-and-peer-evaluation--20-points) | Week 16 | 10% | 20 | individual |
| **Total** | | **100%** | **200** | |

**How to read the tables.** Each row is one criterion with its maximum points. The four columns describe what work at each level looks like; the grader picks the level that fits best and a whole-number score in its range. "Excellent" does not mean perfect — it means complete, correct, specific, and honest about limits.

**Team grades and individual contribution.** Team milestones give every member the same score, with one exception. If **at least two teammates** rate a member's share of the work **below 60% of an equal share** (below 15% in a team of 4, below 20% in a team of 3) on the [peer evaluation](templates/peer-evaluation.md), **and** the check-in record ("Contributions so far") agrees, that member's team-milestone points are multiplied by a factor **between 0.5 and 1.0**, chosen by the instructor from the evidence. The student is told first and can respond before grades are final.

## How the rubrics map to course and ABET outcomes

The project is the main place the course measures ABET student outcomes (SO) 3 and 5. Rows tagged `SO n` in the tables below are the evidence. For ABET reporting, the instructor records the share of students scoring at least 70% of the points on each tagged row (target: 70% of students).

| ABET student outcome | Course outcome | Rubric rows that measure it |
|---|---|---|
| **SO 3** — communicate effectively with a range of audiences | CLO8 | Presentation: *Delivery*, *Questions* · Report: *Writing, figures, and length* |
| **SO 4** — ethical and professional responsibility, and the impact of engineering in context | CLO7 | Proposal: *Verification and responsible-AI first look* · Presentation: *Responsible-AI analysis* · Report: *Responsible-AI analysis* |
| **SO 5** — function effectively on a team | CLO8 | Proposal: *Plan, roles, and team charter* · Check-in: *Plan, blockers, and contributions* · Notebook + report: *Team plan executed* · Peer evaluation: both rows |
| **SO 6** — experimentation, data analysis, and engineering judgment | CLO2, CLO3, CLO7 | Proposal: *Method and baseline*, *Evaluation plan* · Check-in: *Baseline running* · Presentation: *Method and baseline*, *Results and evaluation*, *Verification* · Report: *Method*, *Baseline and evaluation*, *Verification* |
| **SO 7** — acquire and apply new knowledge | CLO4, CLO6 | Reflection: *Technical learning* |

Track-specific outcomes (CLO2 Predict, CLO3 See, CLO4–CLO5 Assist, CLO4 Evaluate) are measured through the *Method* and *Results/Baseline and evaluation* rows of your track's project.

## 1. Proposal — 20 points

Template: [proposal canvas](templates/proposal-canvas.md) (two pages maximum) + [team charter](templates/team-charter.md). Due Sunday of Week 11, 11:59 pm.

| Criterion (points) | Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning / missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem, question, and users** (4) | **4** — Specific, measurable question; named user and decision; clear why it matters. | **3** — Clear question and user; the decision or importance is vague. | **2** — A topic rather than a question ("AI for bridges"); user unclear. | **0–1** — Missing, or not an engineering problem. |
| **Data and its limits** (4) | **4** — Source, license, size, what one row is, inputs/target with units; at least two specific limits; data confirmed accessible (or, for Assist/Evaluate, a test-set plan with an answer key). | **3** — Data identified and accessible; limits general or units missing. | **2** — Data source named but not checked, license unknown, or limits missing. | **0–1** — No usable data, or data that breaks the data policy. |
| **Method and baseline** (3) `SO 6` | **3** — Method fits the problem and data size; closest lab named; a fair baseline is defined. | **2** — Reasonable method and a baseline, with little justification. | **1** — Method vague ("use AI") or baseline missing. | **0** — No method. |
| **Evaluation plan and success criterion** (3) `SO 6` | **3** — Split/test-set plan keeps test data unseen; metrics fit the task and how serious a mistake would be, with units; a concrete success criterion. | **2** — Metrics named; split or success criterion unclear. | **1** — "We will check accuracy" with no detail. | **0** — Missing. |
| **Verification and responsible-AI first look** (3) `SO 4` | **3** — A plan for each of the five checks; specific risks, affected people, and accountability. | **2** — Most checks planned; risks general. | **1** — One or two vague lines. | **0** — Missing. |
| **Plan, roles, and team charter** (3) `SO 5` | **3** — Week-by-week plan to Week 16 with leads; realistic scope; a backup plan for the biggest risk; charter complete, specific, and signed by all. | **2** — Plan and charter present; roles, backup plan, or charter details missing. | **1** — One-line plan, or charter missing. | **0** — Plan and charter missing. |

## 2. Progress check-in — 20 points

| Criterion (points) | Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning / missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Data loaded, cleaned, explored** (5) | **5** — Notebook loads the data with `get_data()` or a documented download; cleaning recorded with before/after counts; labeled plots with units; data card started. | **4** — Data loads and is explored; cleaning or data card incomplete. | **2–3** — Data loads but little exploration, or cleaning undocumented. | **0–1** — Data does not load. |
| **Baseline running with a test metric** (6) `SO 6` | **6** — Baseline runs from a fresh restart; correct split or test set; metric reported in units on unseen data; plausibility checked. | **4–5** — Baseline runs; small issues (training-set metric only, units missing). | **2–3** — Baseline partly working or not reproducible. | **0–1** — No baseline. |
| **Progress on the main method or test set** (3) | **3** — First version of the main method runs, or the full test set and answer key are built (Assist/Evaluate). | **2** — Clear, specific plan with some code started. | **1** — Vague plan only. | **0** — Nothing. |
| **Verification and responsible AI started** (3) | **3** — At least two checks done with results noted; risk notes started. | **2** — One check done; risk notes started. | **1** — Planned only. | **0** — Missing. |
| **Plan, blockers, and contributions** (3) `SO 5` | **3** — Realistic plan to Week 15 with owners; blocker and needed help named; contributions table complete; every member can explain the project. | **2** — Plan present; contributions incomplete, or one member cannot explain their part. | **1** — Plan vague; contributions very uneven or unclear. | **0** — No plan; team not present. |

| Criterion (points) | Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning / missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Team: complete story** (3) | **3** — Every required element appears at least briefly: problem/users, data and limits, method, baseline, results, verification, responsible AI, takeaway. | **2** — One or two elements missing. | **1** — Mostly background; few results. | **0** — Did not present. |
| **Team: on time, readable, everyone speaks** (3) | **3** — Finishes between 4:30 and 5:30; slides readable; at least one real result shown; every member speaks. | **2** — One of these is missing. | **1** — Two or more are missing. | **0** — Not ready. |
| **Individual: peer-feedback forms** (4) | **4** — Both assigned forms complete; comments specific, tied to rubric criteria, actionable, and respectful ("Slide 6: the MAE has no units — add MPa"). | **3** — Both forms complete; comments mostly specific. | **2** — One form missing, or both general ("Good job!"). | **0–1** — No forms, or disrespectful comments. |

## 4. Final presentation — 60 points

Guide: [presentation guide](templates/presentation-guide.md). **10 minutes** + 3–5 minutes of questions (or **8 minutes** if the posted schedule says your room uses 8-minute talks). The moderator stops you at the time limit.

| Criterion (points) | Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning / missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem, users, motivation** (8) | **8** — Real engineering situation; named user and decision; a measurable question; the audience knows why it matters within the first minute. | **6–7** — Clear problem; user or decision vague. | **4–5** — A topic, not a question; long background. | **0–3** — Unclear what the project is about. |
| **Data and its limits** (8) | **8** — Source, license, size, inputs/target with units; a useful exploratory figure; specific limits explained and connected to results. | **6–7** — Data described; limits general. | **4–5** — Data barely described; no limits. | **0–3** — Missing. |
| **Method and baseline** (10) `SO 6` | **9–10** — Method clearly explained (a diagram helps) and justified; fair baseline; explains how the test stayed unseen. | **7–8** — Method and baseline clear; little justification. | **4–6** — Method vague or baseline missing. | **0–3** — Missing. |
| **Results and evaluation** (10) `SO 6` | **9–10** — Baseline vs method on test data in units; metrics fit how serious a mistake would be; real failure cases or subgroup errors shown; honest interpretation. | **7–8** — Clear results vs baseline; little failure analysis. | **4–6** — Results without baseline, units, or a fitting metric (for example, accuracy only on imbalanced data). | **0–3** — No results. |
| **Verification** (6) `SO 6` | **6** — At least two checks shown with what each found (including any problem caught); the slide says all five are in the report. | **4–5** — Two checks shown; findings thin. | **2–3** — One check, or checks named without findings. | **0–1** — Missing. |
| **Responsible-AI analysis** (8) `SO 4` | **8** — Specific risks and failure modes; who could be harmed or left out; where it should not be used; who is accountable and what human review is required. | **6–7** — Relevant but partly general. | **4–5** — A general list ("bias is a risk"). | **0–3** — Missing, or claims the tool is ready for real decisions. |
| **Delivery: clarity, slides, timing** (6) `SO 3` | **6** — Plain language; slide titles that state the finding; readable charts with units; every member speaks; smooth switches between speakers; ends between 9:00 and 10:00 (7:00–8:00 for 8-minute talks). | **4–5** — Mostly clear; some crowded slides; ends early by 1–2 minutes. | **2–3** — Reads from slides; hard-to-read charts; stopped by the moderator before the takeaway, or more than 2 minutes short. | **0–1** — Disorganized; most sections missing. |
| **Questions** (4) `SO 3` | **4** — Accurate, honest answers from several members; says "we did not test that" instead of guessing. | **3** — Mostly good answers; one member answers most questions. | **2** — Vague or partly wrong answers. | **0–1** — Cannot answer basic questions about their own project. |

## 5. Final notebook and report — 70 points

Templates: [report template](templates/report-template.md), [starter notebook](Project_Starter_Notebook.ipynb), [AI-use statement](templates/ai-use-statement.md). Report ≤ 6 pages; graders stop reading at page 6.

### Report (54 points)

| Criterion (points) | Excellent | Good | Developing | Beginning / missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem statement and users** (5) | **5** — Specific question, named user and decision, why it matters, success criterion; the summary states the main result with numbers. | **4** — Clear; user, decision, or success criterion vague. | **2–3** — A topic rather than a question. | **0–1** — Missing. |
| **Data and its limits** (8) | **8** — Complete data card (source, license, size, what one row is, units); cleaning documented with counts; exploratory figure with caption; specific limits; answer key checked (Assist/Evaluate). | **6–7** — Mostly complete; limits or cleaning thin. | **4–5** — Data described loosely; few limits. | **0–3** — Missing, or uses data the policy does not allow. |
| **Method** (8) `SO 6` | **8** — Detail another team could repeat; justified choice; split or test design keeps test data unseen (time or group splits where needed); what did not work is reported. | **6–7** — Clear; light justification, or a minor leakage risk not discussed. | **4–5** — Hard to repeat; test design unclear. | **0–3** — Missing, or test data used for training/tuning. |
| **Baseline and evaluation with appropriate metrics** (10) `SO 6` | **9–10** — Fair baseline; metrics fit the task and how serious a mistake would be (units, recall for rare classes, correctness rate); results table and key figure; at least two real failure cases or subgroup errors; honest interpretation. | **7–8** — Baseline and metrics sound; little failure analysis. | **4–6** — Missing baseline, units, or a fitting metric. | **0–3** — No evaluation. |

| **Responsible-AI analysis** (10) `SO 4` | **9–10** — Specific risks and failure modes and their costs; bias and representativeness with evidence (for example, subgroup errors); distribution shift; accountability and required human review; short model/tool card. | **7–8** — Covers most items; some general. | **4–6** — A general list not tied to this project. | **0–3** — Missing, or presents the tool as safe for real design, inspection, or safety decisions. |
| **Writing, figures, and length** (5) `SO 3` | **5** — ≤ 6 pages; plain, short sentences; terms defined; every figure titled, labeled with units, captioned; sources cited. | **4** — Minor clarity or figure issues. | **2–3** — Hard to follow; unlabeled figures; missing citations. | **0–1** — Disorganized or far over length. |
